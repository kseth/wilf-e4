\section{How the proof was constructed}\label{sec:construction}

Karthik Sethuraman began this project through repeated iterations with
GPT-6 Astra over a period of several weeks. Those iterations produced
a complete but difficult-to-read candidate proof, accompanied by numerous
computational and exploratory artifacts. The initial document was a
starting point for reconstruction, rather than a ready manuscript.

Once that candidate was available, Sethuraman iterated with GPT-5.6 Sol
via Codex to break apart, simplify, rephrase, and improve the proof.
These repository-based iterations decomposed the argument into small
proof obligations and explicit dependencies. We reconstructed
the Ap\'ery foundations, separated the geometric branches, normalized
notation and endpoints, and audited the final arithmetic. We simplified
the argument, removed unnecessary conditions, and replaced computations
with analytic proofs where we could. In particular, the three-plane
branch became entirely analytic, the low short-corner branch retained
only two explicit centroid witnesses instead of a saved dual list, and
the degree-six branch used complete local-or-axis predicates instead
of a saved fallback family. The mesh transfer was reduced to the three
allowances that occur almost everywhere.

Literature work formed part of this reconstruction. We identified and
attributed the preferred-factorization, weighted-downset, initial-ideal,
and support ingredients; checked the exact published multiplicity
cutoff; and assessed whether Chomicz's results could simplify the
retained argument. We also examined what survives in more exponent
coordinates and where the three-dimensional structure fails. These
assessments shaped both the proof and its stated scope.

Computer assistance has two distinct roles here. AI assisted discovery,
reconstruction, exposition, and checking. Exact programs establish the
seven finite assertions specified mathematically in the paper, as
described in Section~\ref{sec:verification}. Neither AI-generated text
nor agreement of two programs is itself a mathematical justification
for a reduction.

The present draft records an internally reconstructed and replayed
computer-assisted argument. Independent specialist review and any
decision to formalize selected components remain subsequent steps.
