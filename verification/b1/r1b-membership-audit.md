## 1. Ordinary membership recurrence

For a generator prefix \(G_j\), let \(I_j(n)\) state that \(n\) belongs to
the monoid generated by \(G_j\). When a positive generator \(w\) is adjoined,

\[
I_j(n)=I_{j-1}(n)\ \vee\
\bigl(n\ge w\text{ and }I_j(n-w)\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]

The alternatives distinguish representations using zero copies of \(w\)
from those using at least one; removing one copy proves the reverse
implication. With \(I_j(0)=1\), increasing evaluation of \(n\) is therefore
exact by induction.

The source first computes `ra` for \(\langle m,a\rangle\), then computes
`rab` for \(\langle m,a,b\rangle\). Consequently `ra[b]` and `rab[c]` are
exactly the two nontrivial prefix-redundancy predicates. Every array entry
from 1 through \(B_m\) is assigned on every relevant pass, so reuse of the
allocated vectors cannot import stale membership state.

After the prefix tests, \(\gcd(m,a,b,c)=1\) is exactly the remaining condition
for cofiniteness. As in the specification, the ordered tests give a disjoint
classification, and later larger generators cannot make an earlier generator
redundant.

## 2. Packed final-window test

For fixed \(m,a,b\), the source packs the membership indicators of
\(\langle m,a,b\rangle\cap[0,B_m]\) into 64-bit blocks. Its `window` helper
returns an \(m\)-bit word whose bit \(t\) is one exactly when

\[
B_m-m+1+t-kc\in\langle m,a,b\rangle
\]

for the requested shift \(kc\), treating negative indices as absent. Taking
the bitwise union over \(kc\le B_m\) therefore sets bit \(t\) exactly when

\[
B_m-m+1+t\in\langle m,a,b,c\rangle.
\]

No larger shift can contribute to a nonnegative target at most \(B_m\). Thus
the union is the full mask precisely when

\[
[B_m-m+1,B_m]\subseteq\langle m,a,b,c\rangle.
\tag{2}
\]

By the window equivalence in the B1.3 specification, (2) is equivalent to
the Apéry maximum satisfying \(M\le B_m\).

The width obeys \(20\le m\le29<64\), so `(1ULL << m) - 1` is defined. The
packed vector has two guard blocks; every `block + 1` access lies inside it.
When the negative-start shift is reached, its size is at most \(m-1<64\);
starts at most \(-m\) return before shifting. The runner also checks the
packed extraction formula against its closed-form answer on every one-bit
basis vector and a superset of all starts that can affect an in-scope window.
All 3,329,978 such basis cases passed.

## 3. Conductor, genus, and Wilf number

For a tuple passing (2), the source applies (1) once more to compute full
membership in \(\langle m,a,b,c\rangle\) throughout \([0,B_m]\). Since the
last \(m\) entries are present, adding multiples of \(m\) proves that every
integer above \(B_m\) is present as well. Hence the number of absent positive
integers in the table is the full genus \(g\), and one plus the final absent
integer is the conductor \(C\).

The program then evaluates

\[
W_4=3C-4g.
\tag{3}
\]

It also reconstructs \(M=C+m-1\) and rejects if \(M>B_m\), providing an
internal check of the packed-window implication. The Apéry sum reconstructed
as

\[
\Sigma=mg+\frac{m(m-1)}2
\]

is used only in the diagnostic checksum.

## 4. Exhaustive generation and arithmetic safety

The nested loops have the inclusive bounds

\[
m+1\le a\le B_m-2,
\quad a+1\le b\le B_m-1,
\quad b+1\le c\le B_m.
\]

They independently regenerate every element of \(\mathcal R_m\); no tuple
stream or classification is imported from R1a. Bulk counts after a rejected
prefix account for exactly all increasing completions. The source and runner
both check that the rejection categories and valid category partition

\[
\sum_{m=20}^{29}\binom{B_m-m}{3}=301{,}098{,}092
\]

raw triples.

Here \(B_m\le783\). Membership indices and vector sizes therefore fit in
`int`. In the required domain, \(C\le B_m-m+1\), \(g\le B_m\), and all
calculations in (3) fit easily in signed 32-bit integers. The diagnostic
Apéry sum fits in signed 64-bit integers. Counts use unsigned 64-bit integers.
Overflow of the unsigned FNV accumulator is defined by C++ and affects only a
diagnostic checksum.

## 5. Failure behavior and fresh replay

The historical executable reports aggregate counts rather than exiting when
it finds a negative Wilf value. The new runner supplies the fail-closed
boundary. It rejects compilation or execution failure, compiler warnings
under `-Werror`, stderr output, missing or extra output records, malformed
JSON or diagnostics, an unexpected schema or multiplicity, a wrong endpoint
or raw count, a failed category partition, an invalid bounded count, or any
negative count or minimum. It writes a failure record and exits nonzero on
any such condition.

## 7. Independent agreement and trust boundary

After R1b independently passed, the runner opened the successful R1a record.
For every multiplicity, the two paths agree on the generator bound, raw and
classification counts, number of valid tuples, number with \(M\le B_m\),
bounded minimum and witness, and checksum of every bounded tuple's
\((a,b,c,M,\Sigma,W_4)\) data in traversal order.

The negative and zero counts are deliberately not used as agreement fields:
R1a records them over all valid boxed tuples, while R1b records them only in
the required \(M\le B_m\) domain. Each path independently validated its own
negative count as zero.

The combined B1 result trusts the B1.2 reduction and B1.3 specification, two
inspected C++ implementations with distinct invariant calculations, the two
fail-closed Python runners, the C++17 and Python implementations, compiler,
operating system, and hardware. It does not trust either historical result
file. This supplies the independent checking path required by V0; it is not
external peer review or proof-assistant formalization.

The implementations necessarily share the canonical sorted generator box,
the prefix-redundancy/gcd classification, and the checksum convention from
the theorem specification. Their derivations of membership, \(M\), genus, and
the Wilf number are materially different. This is independent code and
algorithmic representation under V0, not independent authorship and not a
check of the analytic reduction defining the finite domain.
