# Exact exhaustion of four-generator multiplicities 20 through 29

Research checkpoint: 7 September 2026.

**Theorem.** Every numerical semigroup of embedding dimension four and
multiplicity `20 <= m <= 29` satisfies Wilf's inequality.

This is a complete finite verification combined with the previously proved
analytic conductor reduction. It includes both the no-interior-corner and
one-interior-corner Apéry classes. It is not a proof for arbitrary multiplicity,
and no publication-priority claim is made.

## 1. The finite domain and its justification

Write the unique increasing minimal generator list as

\[
S=\langle m,a,b,c\rangle,\qquad m<a<b<c.
\]

Let `C` be the conductor, `N=|S intersect [0,C)|`, and `W=4N-C`.
Set `M=max Ap(S,m)=C+m-1`. The previously proved analytic conductor reduction is

\[
W<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M\le m(m-2),\qquad c\le M\le m(m-2).
\tag{1}
\]

The proof is §4 of
`deliverables/wilf_edim4_global_finite_reduction_2026-09-05.md`.
I inspected that full argument for the present computation. It has no
large-multiplicity hypothesis. Its relevant proof chain is as follows.

* A preferred three-variable Apéry lower ideal has residue-bijective labels
  and at most one full-support minimal excluded exponent.
* A full weighted Apéry ideal has one of three possible two-coordinate
  projections. Residue arithmetic excludes the six-column triangular
  projection. A boundary inequality proves `W>=0` for the remaining
  three-column and four-column projections.
* If `W<0`, therefore, the preferred ideal has a minimal excluded exponent
  `p` of weight at most `M`. Its distinct predecessor coordinate lines
  contribute at least `M` to the nonnegative line-deficit identity
  `D=3mM-4 sum Ap(S,m)`. In detail, their contribution is
  `M+(|p|_1-1)(M-w(p)) >= M`.
* The exact genus identity is `mW=D-m(m-1)`. Integrality of negative `W`
  gives `D<=m(m-2)`, proving (1). Every nonmultiplicity minimal generator
  is an Apéry element, so its value is at most `M`.

Consequently it is sufficient, for each integer `m=20,...,29`, to examine
**every** integer triple

\[
m<a<b<c\le B_m,\qquad B_m=m(m-2).
\tag{2}
\]

The right endpoint is included. Triples giving redundant generators or
nontrivial total gcd are not minimally four-generated numerical semigroups.
All other triples are included. Equation (1) handles the infinite complement
of (2); it does not rely on a heuristic restriction of the search.

## 2. First complete algorithm: cyclic residue shortest paths

The program `exhaust_small_multiplicity.cpp` iterates all triples (2), in
lexicographic order. It represents a semigroup generated by `m` and a prefix
of the other generators by its minimum attainable value in each residue
modulo `m`, with infinity for unreachable residues.

When adjoining a generator `w`, the exact new distance is

\[
d'(r)=\min_{0\le k<L}\{d(r-kw)+kw\},
\quad L=m/\gcd(m,w).
\tag{3}
\]

Only `k<L` is needed: an additional full residue cycle increases the value
by the positive number `Lw`. Translation by `w` consists of disjoint
directed cycles. Two complete forward relaxation passes on each cycle
propagate each possible old source through every vertex following it;
thus they compute (3) exactly. Every relaxation is a feasible path, so
the result cannot underestimate the shortest path.

Ascending generator order permits exact redundancy tests against the
previous prefix: later positive generators cannot represent a smaller
earlier generator. Total gcd one is equivalent to eventual reachability
of every residue. For every surviving tuple, the program calculates

\[
W=\frac{3mM-4\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}d(r)-m(m-1)}m.
\tag{4}
\]

The numerator's divisibility by `m` is checked. There are no floating-point
comparisons in the enumeration or in the inequality check. A count partition
accounts for every triple, including the exact numbers omitted by each
redundancy or gcd test.

## 3. Second complete algorithm: integer membership

The separate program `independent_membership_check.cpp` iterates the same
complete domain using ordinary integer membership on `[0,B_m]`. It does
not call the first program or use residue shortest paths.

For a generator prefix, membership is computed by the standard positive
integer recurrence. On adjoining `c`,

\[
1_S(n)=1_{\langle m,a,b\rangle}(n)
\;\mathbin{\mathrm{or}}\;
(n\ge c\text{ and }1_S(n-c)).
\]

Before computing the full membership vector, the program obtains exact
membership of `[B_m-m+1,B_m]` as the union of translates of the prefix
membership vector. Bit operations merely evaluate this finite Boolean union.

The following equivalence justifies the only substantive pruning step:

\[
M\le B_m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[B_m-m+1,B_m]\subseteq S.
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed, the interval contains the largest integer at most `B_m` in each
residue class. If every residue has its minimum representative at most
`B_m`, its interval representative belongs to `S` by closure under addition
of `m`. Conversely, presence of every interval representative supplies a
representative of every residue at most `B_m`.

When (5) holds, all integers larger than `B_m` also belong to `S`, by closure
under addition of `m`. Thus direct integer membership yields the **full**
genus `g`, the last gap and conductor `C`, and independently the Wilf number

\[
W=3C-4g.
\]

This algorithm checks every possible counterexample by (1). Both programs
produce identical counts and identical checksums of the complete sequence
`(a,b,c,M,sum Ap,W)` for every tuple with `M<=B_m`. Checksums are reproducibility
diagnostics; exact enumeration and the algorithm proofs supply the certificate.

the entire checked region contains none, the theorem follows.

## 5. Reproduction and precise scope

Files:

* `exhaust_small_multiplicity.cpp`: full generator-box enumeration.
* `independent_membership_check.cpp`: independent membership enumeration.
* `exhaustive_m20_m29.jsonl`, `independent_m20_m29.jsonl`: complete per-m
  count certificates and checksums.
* `verify_exhaustion.py`: compares certificates, validates all displayed
  minimizing examples by an additional Python membership calculation,
  checks count partitions and totals, and optionally rebuilds/reruns both
  enumerations.
* `exhaustion_verification.json`: verification output and source hashes.

Run from any directory with Python 3 and a C++17 compiler:

```sh
python3 /path/to/small_multiplicity/verify_exhaustion.py --rerun
```

The result removes `20<=m<=29` from **all** residual four-generator cases.
With the prior published verification through multiplicity 19, a remaining
four-generator counterexample must have `m>=30`.

The separate completed Round 5 weighted no-interior-corner theorem proves
the needed inequality for every no-interior preferred Apéry ideal with
`m>=30`. Combining that theorem, the present exact exhaustion, and the
published `m<=19` result therefore settles **the entire no-interior-corner
class**. The old interval `30<=m<=1836` is no longer residual in that class.

The present computation itself still has exactly the interval `20<=m<=29`
as its scope. It does not enumerate the remaining one-interior-corner
class at `m>=30`.
