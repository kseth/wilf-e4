# Independent audit of the multiplicity 20–29 exhaustion

7 September 2026. Verdict: **PASS**, conditional on the already proved
four-generator negative-case bound `M<=m(m-2)`. This audit checked the new
enumeration's logic and the matching results of two different algorithms; it
did not rerun both full enumerations for a third time.

The relevant prior bound is the sharper four-generator result in
`prior/wilf_edim4_uniform_conductor_reduction_2026-09-05.md`, Sections 3–5,
also independently audited in `round4/audit/independent_finite_reduction_audit.md`,
Section 6. It is stronger than the separate general-dimension conductor bound.
Every negative Wilf semigroup satisfies

\[
M\le m(m-2),\qquad m<a<b<c\le M.
\]

## Coverage and pruning

The first program lists exactly the strictly increasing triples
`m<a<b<c<=m(m-2)`. Its outer and inner endpoint conditions are inclusive where
required. It discards a generator precisely when the generator is in the
semigroup generated by its smaller predecessors. A larger future generator
cannot make a smaller one redundant because every generator is positive.
Consequently these tests characterize minimal four-element generating sets.
The final gcd test discards precisely nonnumerical semigroups.

The recorded raw triple count partitions into the three redundancy categories,
the nontrivial-gcd category, and retained semigroups. Both implementations
independently reproduce that partition for every multiplicity.

## First algorithm: adjoining a generator on residue cycles

Before adjoining `w`, the array gives the minimum nonnegative value at each
residue using the earlier generators. All mixed sums may be reordered, so
after adjoining `w` the required value is the minimum of an earlier value
plus `k*w`. Translation by `w mod m` splits the residues into gcd cycles.
Since `w>0`, a shortest candidate uses at most one cycle less one edge. Two
full forward passes over each cycle propagate every possible starting value
to every possible endpoint. The in-place update is therefore exact.

The code's cycle starts `0,...,gcd(m,w)-1` cover all cycles, and the second
pass includes every wraparound case. The positive infinity sentinel cannot
become a false finite value. At these input sizes both finite distances and
sentinel additions are far below signed integer overflow; sums and the Wilf
numerator use 64-bit integers.

The genus/Apéry identity yields the exact integer Wilf number

\[
W_4=[3mM-4\sum\operatorname{Ap}(S,m)-m(m-1)]/m.
\]

The code checks divisibility by `m` for every retained tuple.

## Second algorithm: direct integer membership

The other program computes membership in `<m,a>` and `<m,a,b>` directly on
the integer interval `0,...,B`, where `B=m(m-2)`. Its recurrence uses only
smaller integer indices and computes all entries afresh.

For a candidate `c`, shifting the `<m,a,b>` membership bitset by every
nonnegative multiple of `c` gives exact membership in `<m,a,b,c>` on the
last `m` values `B-m+1,...,B`. The window extraction handles partial negative
indices by zero padding, and its shifts have width less than 64 in the
advertised multiplicity range. Allocated extra words make both-word reads
valid at the upper endpoint.

That last window is completely present exactly when the actual maximum
Apéry value is at most `B`. Indeed a full window and closure under `+m` give
conductor at most `B-m+1`; the converse follows from the conductor formula.
Thus the second program discards only cases excluded by the prior necessary
bound. For every surviving tuple it recomputes all integer membership values,
counts the full genus, locates the conductor, and computes `W4=3c-4g`.

The two implementations agree on every reported per-multiplicity count,
minimum, witness, and checksum in their common bounded-Apéry domain. The
checksum is a reproducibility cross-check, not a replacement for the
exhaustive algorithm or its proof.

## Results and scope

`verify_exhaustion.py` passed during this audit. Its witness validation uses
another elementary integer membership recurrence. The recorded totals are

* 301,098,092 raw increasing generator triples;
* 180,719,029 minimally four-generated numerical semigroups in the generator
  box;
* 7,326,992 of those with maximum Apéry value within the necessary bound;
* zero negative Wilf numbers.

Therefore **every four-generated numerical semigroup of multiplicity 20
through 29 satisfies Wilf**, not only the no-interior subclass.

The computation also finds no zero Wilf value in the finite box. This does
not prove strict positivity for all semigroups of those multiplicities,
because the necessary bound was established for negative Wilf numbers.
The justified unrestricted conclusion here is `W4>=0`.

Together with the published result through multiplicity 19 and the new
weighted no-interior theorem for `m>=30`, the argument proves Wilf for the
entire no-interior preferred-Apéry class. The one-interior-corner class at
multiplicity at least 30 remains outside these results.
