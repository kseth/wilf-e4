# Independent audit of the global Wilf reductions

Audit date: 9 September 2026.

## Verdict and scope

I read the arguments in the two manuscripts below as mathematical proofs, rather than relying on their previous verification statements:

1. `deliverables/wilf_edim4_global_finite_reduction_2026-09-05.md`.
2. `deliverables/wilf_fixed_dimension_finite_reduction_2026-09-05.md`.

I found no logical gap in the preferred Apéry ideal construction, the one-full-support-corner restriction, the analytic four-generator conductor reduction, or the improved large-multiplicity argument. The latter establishes the stated sufficient cutoff \(m\ge 2\cdot10^{14}\). The general fixed-dimension reduction also passed the audit described below.

This audit is an independent in-session mathematical review. It is not a formal verification, an external referee report, or a proof that the remaining finite region is empty. No numerical search is used as evidence for a universal theorem in this audit.

**Historical correction:** the older manuscript states \(m\ge 10^{27}\), not \(m\ge1027\). The visible text `1027` lost the exponent formatting. The newer cutoff \(2\cdot10^{14}\) is a genuine improvement of \(10^{27}\); the two manuscript statements do not conflict. The visible historical claim with multiplicity 1027 should not be propagated as a theorem of these sources.

## 1. Apéry construction and arithmetic structure

Let \(S=\langle m,a_1,a_2,a_3\rangle\) be minimally four-generated with \(m<a_1<a_2<a_3\). For each Apéry element, choose its lexicographically least factorization in the three nonmultiplicity generators. Positive weights give finitely many factorizations of each fixed integer, so the selection exists.

If \(x\) is selected and \(y\le x\) coordinatewise, then \(w(y)\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,m)\): otherwise \(w(y)-m\in S\), and adding \(w(x-y)\) contradicts the Apéry property of \(w(x)\). A lexicographically smaller factorization of \(w(y)\) could likewise be added to \(x-y\) to contradict the selection of \(x\). Thus the selected set \(T\) is a lower ideal. It has \(m\) elements, its labels are bijective modulo \(m\), and minimal generation ensures that all three unit vectors belong to \(T\).

If \(p\notin T\) is minimal excluded and \(q\in T\) is its residue representative, then a shared positive coordinate \(j\) would give distinct \(p-e_j,q-e_j\in T\) with identical residues. Hence their supports are disjoint. Similarly, two distinct minimal excluded points with intersecting supports have distinct residues. A full-support excluded point must therefore represent zero, and there is at most one such point. This argument also works in arbitrary finite dimension.

The coordinate-line and genus identities were checked independently. With

\[
m=|T|,\quad M=\max_T w=c+m-1,\quad \Sigma=\sum_Tw,
\quad D=3mM-4\Sigma,
\]

they are

\[
D=\sum_{j=1}^3\sum_{\ell\parallel e_j}L_\ell(M-w(t_\ell)),
\qquad mW_4=D-m(m-1).
\]

Lines here are indexed by their directions as well as their projections. Every summand is nonnegative. The line identity gives only \(D\ge0\), whereas Wilf requires \(D\ge m(m-1)\).

## 2. Full weighted ideals: classification and exclusion

Suppose \(T=\{x\in\mathbb N^3:w(x)\le M\}\), with weights \(a\le b\le d\), and put \(h_2=\lfloor M/b\rfloor\), \(h_3=\lfloor M/d\rfloor\). For every \(1\le y\le h_2\),

\[
p_y=(\lfloor(M-by)/a\rfloor+1,y,0)
\]

is minimal excluded. Both predecessor tests follow from \(b\ge a\); this is where weight ordering matters. These points must have distinct representatives on the third axis, so \(h_2\le h_3+1\).

If \(M<b+d\), the last-two-coordinate projection has no mixed point, \(h_3=1\), and \(h_2\le2\). This gives precisely three or four columns.

If \(M\ge b+d\), there is an interior minimal excluded point above projection \((1,1)\). It uses zero residue, so no \(p_y\) can use zero and \(h_2\le h_3\). A second interior point would arise if \(M\ge2b+d\), which is impossible. Thus \(h_3\le2\le h_2\le h_3\). It follows that \(2d\le M<3b\), and the projection is exactly the six-point triangle \(y+z\le2\).

For this triangle, denote the column lengths at \((0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(0,1),(1,1),(0,2)\) by \(A,B,C,F,E,G\). Full weightedness gives the strict descents required by the manuscript. Let \(r\) be the additive order of \(a\) modulo \(m\). The interior relation is

\[
Ea+b+d=0.
\]

The origin and mixed columns lie in the subgroup generated by \(a\), contain \(A+E\) distinct residues, and give \(r\ge A+E\). The representatives of the two \(xy\)-plane corners must permute \(d,2d\). The swapped assignment gives \((B-C+E)a=0\), with \(0<B-C+E<r\), which is impossible. The unswapped assignment and its symmetric counterpart imply that

\[
B+F,\quad 2B-C,\quad 2F-G
\]

are positive multiples of \(r\), all less than \(2A\le2r\). Each therefore equals \(r\). Adding the last two and comparing with twice the first yields \(C+G=0\), contradicting positivity. I checked that the same strict order argument rules out the swapped assignment in the symmetric plane. Thus the six-column case is excluded without enumeration.

## 3. Boundary inequality for the surviving columns

Let

\[
Z=\{x\in T:M-w(x)<m\},\quad k=|Z|,
\quad P(T)=\sum_j|\pi_jT|,
\]

\[
E(Z)=\sum_{z\in Z}|z|_1-\sum_j\max_{z\in Z}z_j.
\]

The set \(Z\) is nonempty, and all its points are maximal because each generator exceeds \(m\). The claimed estimate is

\[
mW_4\ge m\bigl(P(T)-3k-E(Z)\bigr)+E(Z).
\]

The residue accounting is sound. On a line with top deficit \(mq+\beta\), its deficit residues are the residues of \(\beta+s a_j\), for \(0\le s<L\). Writing \(R=\sum_s[s a_j]_m\) and counting carries gives its contribution in the manuscript's exact rearrangement. If the top is outside \(Z\), then \(q\ge1\). The \(L\) axis residues are distinct, one is zero, and

\[
R\le m(L-1)-\binom L2.
\]

Consequently that line's rearranged contribution is at least \(m\). There are \(P(T)-3k\) such lines.

For the remaining \(3k\) lines, discard the nonnegative carry contributions. The budget \(\binom m2\) pays for every distinct positive axis residue once. These are distinct even between different coordinate axes because they label different points of \(T\). The number of unpaid repeated copies is exactly \(E(Z)\), and each costs at most \(m-1\). This proves the inequality, including its signs and the coefficient of \(E(Z)\).

For \(K=\operatorname{Max}(T)\), both \(|Z|\le|K|\) and \(E(Z)\le E(K)\). In the three-column case direct counting gives

\[
P(T)-3|K|-E(K)=2A-4\ge0;
\]

in the four-column case it gives \(2A-6\ge0\). Strict column descents ensure that all column tops are maximal and that \(A\ge2\) or \(A\ge3\), respectively. Therefore every full weighted Apéry ideal satisfies Wilf.

## 4. The strong conductor reduction

If a preferred Apéry ideal is not full weighted, choose an omitted point with weight at most \(M\), and descend to a minimal excluded point \(p\) below it. Then \(w(p)\le M\).

For every \(j\) with \(p_j>0\), the line through \(p-e_j\) in direction \(j\) has length \(p_j\) and top \(p-e_j\). Its contribution to \(D\) is \(p_j(M-w(p)+a_j)\). Summing these lines gives exactly

\[
\sum_{j:p_j>0}p_j(M-w(p)+a_j)
=M+(|p|_1-1)(M-w(p))\ge M.
\]

There is no overlap error: in any fixed direction, two different minimal excluded points cannot determine the same predecessor line, because they would be comparable. Lines with different directions are different indexed summands of the coordinate-line identity, even if they contain common points.

If \(W_4<0\), the full weighted theorem forces the non-full case. Since \(W_4\) is an integer, \(mW_4\le-m\), and hence

\[
M\le D=mW_4+m(m-1)\le m(m-2).
\]

Every nonmultiplicity minimal generator is an Apéry element. Therefore

\[
\boxed{W_4<0\implies M\le m(m-2),\quad c\le m^2-3m+1,
\quad a_3\le m(m-2).}
\]

This implication has no large-multiplicity hypothesis and no computer-assisted dependency. It is the appropriate conductor reduction for a finite search at small fixed multiplicities.

## 5. Continuous gap and transfer

I checked both continuous arguments, particularly the sharper constant \(\kappa_c\ge1/10000\). The minimum-coordinate slicing is valid: after fixing the minimum coordinate \(r\), subtracting \(r\) from all other coordinates leaves a planar downset, so its conditional mean is bounded by the planar coordinate-fiber inequality. This gives

\[
\mathbb E(x+y+z)\le\frac23+\mathbb E\min(x,y,z).
\]

Under the contrary hypothesis \(\kappa_c<1/10000\), this supplies the initial cube. Each bootstrap rectangle and each witness rectangle lies below the previously forced point in the other coordinates; omission of the next point bounds the relevant fiber from above. All six displayed contradictions are strictly stronger than \(1/10000\). Their exact values are:

| Step | Exact lower bound for \(\kappa_c\) |
|---|---:|
| Bootstrap 1 | \(174/390625\) |
| Bootstrap 2 | \(147/1250000\) |
| Bootstrap 3 | \(63/500000\) |
| Witness 1 | \(837/781250\) |
| Witness 2 | \(189/500000\) |
| Witness 3 | \(80703/625000000\) |

All seven required witnesses satisfy the stated propagation conditions after a coordinate permutation. Their inclusion forces two omitted outside-simplex points to dominate the same full-support excluded vertex, and hence forces their included coordinatewise minimum to be omitted. The contradiction is valid.

The rectangular thickening preserves the support of every minimal excluded vertex. Its complement is exactly the union of the corresponding closed upper orthants in the nonnegative orthant; the half-open-cell convention is compatible with this assertion. All cells have equal volume. Thus

\[
\kappa_c=\frac{\kappa+s}{1+s}
\]

has the correct discrete-to-continuous correction in dimension three.

I also checked the sharper sawtooth phase argument. The intervals associated to successive points on a fiber tile a single interval of length \(Lv\), rather than overlapping. A remainder interval of length \(r<U\) has sawtooth integral at least \(r^2/2\); Cauchy--Schwarz gives the next bound. The fiber-length-biased averaging uses the exact identity \(v\mathbb E L=2\mu_k+v\). Summing the two phase estimates gives

\[
s(1-3\kappa)\le4\kappa+5v-3\kappa v+4v^2.
\]

The discarded coefficient of the minimum-axis slack is nonpositive because the middle normalized weight is at most one. Assuming \(W_4\le0\) gives \(\kappa<v\), so for \(v<1/3\),

\[
\kappa_c<\frac{2v(5-v)}{1+6v+v^2}<10v.
\]

The lattice count yields \(m\le\binom{\lfloor1/v\rfloor+3}{3}\). At \(m\ge2\cdot10^{14}\), this forces \(v<10^{-5}\), contradicting the continuous gap. Hence the stated strict large-multiplicity theorem is supported.

A minor sharpening follows from the same proof without any new lemma:

\[
m\ge\binom{100003}{3}+1=166676666850002\implies W_4>0.
\]

Indeed \(v\ge10^{-5}\) would give \(m\le\binom{100003}{3}\). This slightly improves the displayed numerical cutoff, but does not materially resolve the remaining problem. It is optional to incorporate it; the manuscript's \(2\cdot10^{14}\) cutoff is valid as written.

## 6. General fixed-dimension reduction

The independent general conductor argument uses a different, weaker estimate, and should not be substituted for the strong four-generator estimate above.

For \(d\ge2,R\ge2\), the difference-set argument for the simplex \(\Delta_R\) is valid: if two points of \(\Delta_{R-1}-\Delta_1\) have equal labels, their cross sums lie in \(\Delta_R\). Injectivity there makes the two differences equal as integer vectors. The count

\[
|\Delta_{R-1}-\Delta_1|-|\Delta_R|
=\frac{(d-1)(R-1)}R\binom{d+R-2}{d-1}>0
\]

then excludes a residue-bijective standard simplex.

The geometric characterization used next is also sound: if every coordinate-line top is maximal, then every unit transfer from a point in \(T\) stays in \(T\). Such transfers connect every fixed-total-degree layer; a largest-degree point forces the entire simplex below that layer. Conversely a standard simplex has every line top maximal.

A non-simplex therefore has a nonmaximal line top with slack at least the smallest weight \(\beta\). Combining it with the smallest-weight axis line, whose length is \(\ell+1\), gives \(D\ge M/\ell\ge M/(m-d)\). If the two lines coincide, the manuscript handles that separately and obtains the stronger \(D\ge M\). The resulting generator bound and the direct maximal-embedding-dimension endpoint both have the correct factors.

The general continuous propagation, phase estimate, and constants also pass algebraic inspection. The full-support corner contradiction uses witnesses of total coordinate at most \(1-h_d\). The conditions on \(\delta_d,P_d,Q_d\) follow directly from their definitions for every integer \(d\ge2\). Thus the general fixed-dimension theorem is supported by the written argument; it does not supply a uniform finite region over all embedding dimensions.

## 7. What this audit does not establish

The audit does not verify the separate six-point theorem, all later interval certificates, or the unrestricted remaining one-interior-corner class. In particular, a global bound on multiplicity and conductor does not establish that no counterexample occurs in the resulting finite region. Claims of a completed four-generator proof require an additional exhaustive or analytic argument for all the residual cases.

No mathematical counterexample to a foundational lemma was found in this audit. The only historical error identified here is the loss of the exponent in `10^{27}` when it was copied into plain text.
