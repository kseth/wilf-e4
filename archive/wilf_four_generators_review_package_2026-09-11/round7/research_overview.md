# Wilf's conjecture for four generators: a computer-assisted proof

Created 10 September 2026; revised 11 September 2026. Research manuscript,
proof sources, and exact certificates.

**The argument in this manuscript proves Wilf's inequality for every
minimally four-generated numerical semigroup.** The last high-height
certificate is complete, and every accepted leaf has been independently
replayed. The low-height certificate and the earlier proof dependencies
have also passed the separate checks described below. There is no remaining
parameter region in the four-generator argument.

This is a computer-assisted research proof, with in-session mathematical
audits and independent exact computations. External mathematical review
and proof-assistant formalization have not been performed. No
publication-priority claim is made, and embedding dimensions greater than
four are not settled by this work.

**Theorem.** If S is a numerical semigroup minimally generated by four
positive integers and c is its conductor, then

\[
\boxed{4|S\cap[0,c)|\ge c.}
\]

## 1. Definitions and the exact target

Let S=<m,a_1,a_2,a_3> be a minimally four-generated numerical semigroup,
with m<a_1<a_2<a_3. Let c be its conductor, n=|S intersect [0,c)|,
and W_4=4n-c. The preferred Apéry exponent ideal T consists of the
lexicographically least factorizations of the elements of Ap(S,m), using
the three nonmultiplicity generators. Put

\[
|T|=m,\qquad M=\max_{x\in T}a\cdot x=c+m-1,
\qquad D=3mM-4\sum_{x\in T}a\cdot x.
\]

The exact Apéry identity is

\[
\boxed{mW_4=D-m(m-1).}
\]

Labels a.x on T are bijective modulo m. A minimal excluded exponent and
its representative in T have disjoint supports: subtracting a shared
positive coordinate would give distinct points of T with equal residues.
Distinct minimal excluded exponents with intersecting supports likewise
have distinct residues. In particular, a full-support excluded exponent
represents zero and there can be at most one such exponent.

An "interior corner" below means a full-support minimal excluded exponent,
not a coordinatewise maximal included point. These two notions must not be
interchanged. Normalized height means H=M/a_1.

## 3. Complete proof by contradiction

Suppose that W_4<0. The multiplicity-through-29 theorem excludes m<=29.
For its new cases m=20,...,29, the analytic conductor reduction proves
that a negative example must satisfy M<=m(m-2) and a_3<=m(m-2).
Two different complete arithmetic enumerations eliminate that domain.
This step covers all generator sizes, rather than assuming a search cutoff.
We may therefore assume m>=30.

The preferred ideal has at most one interior corner by Section 1. The
completed no-interior theorem excludes the case of none. If a corner
exists and has coordinate sum three or four, it is (1,1,1) or a permutation
of (2,1,1). The six-final-window theorem and the short-corner theorem
exclude these cases. The only case to consider is exactly one corner p
with |p|_1>=5.

Set A=a_1>=m+1, b=a/A, H=M/A, and

\[
D_0=D/A=3mH-4\sum_{x\in T}b\cdot x.
\]

The exact identity mW_4=A D_0-m(m-1), together with the integrality of
W_4, gives

\[
D_0\le\frac{m(m-2)}{A}
\le\frac{m(m-2)}{m+1}=m-3+\frac3{m+1}.
\]

Consequently every remaining negative example would satisfy

\[
\boxed{m-D_0\ge\frac{3m}{m+1}\ge\frac{90}{31}
>\frac{29}{10},\qquad
\frac{90}{31}-\frac{29}{10}=\frac1{310}.}
\tag{1}
\]

If H<7, then b_i>=1 implies T is contained in the degree-six simplex.
Minimality of p gives |p|_1<=7. The complete low-height enumeration covers
all nine corner types and every compatible triple of planar projections.
Its filters are necessary consequences of residue injectivity. For every
surviving ideal, an independently checked rational dual proves
D_0>=m-29/10 for every permitted real weight vector and height. The
11 September supplementary certificate covers the 380,607 shapes
previously assigned to the six-final-window theorem, so that theorem is
no longer needed in this residual low-height branch.
This contradicts (1), excluding H<7.

It remains to consider H>=7. Write the sorted weights as (1,b,c), put
S_w=1+b+c, and normalize v=1/H, s=S_w/H, kappa=D_0/(mH).
Coordinate-line slacks give kappa>=0, while (1) gives kappa<v.
The phase inequality and the continuous gap give the necessary conditions

\[
S_w<9+\frac{28}{H-3},\qquad 5H<42+37S_w.
\tag{2}
\]

Combining them yields 5H^2-390H+89<0, hence H<78. The coordinate units
belong to T, so 1<=b<=c<=H. Thus every hypothetical failure lies in the
initial closed real box 1<=b,c<=78, 7<=H<=78.

The final interval certificate covers this entire box. It clips only
parameters inconsistent with weight ordering or the necessary conditions
(2), always rounding endpoints outward. Each split covers its clipped
parent by two closed boxes. Each accepted leaf supplies an exact upper
bound on m-D_0 over all allowed ideals and every real parameter in the box.
The corner enumeration and the central-box/nested-rectangle recurrence
cover all possible p and shapes; no conductor or generator cutoff is
assumed at this step.

The complete tree has 94,459 nodes, consisting of 47,229 splits, 47,088
accepted bound leaves, and 142 empty leaves. There are no pending or
unresolved nodes. Independent traversal checked every clip and split,
and independent integer computations replayed all 47,088 accepted leaves.
Every leaf proves m-D_0<=29/10, again contradicting (1).

Both height branches are excluded. Hence W_4<0 is impossible, proving
W_4>=0 for every minimally four-generated numerical semigroup.

The endpoint H=7 belongs to the high-height branch; H=78 is safely
included in the certificate's closed root box. Coordinate permutations
and multiplicity m=30 are included. The conclusion is nonnegativity;
strict positivity is claimed only for the separately stated subclasses.

## 5. Failed shortcuts and their limits

The new arithmetic condition used in the low-height proof is

\[
|F_i\cap F_j|\le2n_k,\qquad
F_i=\{x\in T:x+e_i\notin T\},\quad n_k=1+\max_Tx_k.
\]

Mixed excluded points in the planar slices inject, by residue labels,
into the complementary coordinate axis. Their number is
|F_i intersect F_j|-n_k, proving the bound. It excludes both shapes that
survived ordinary erosion but failed the unit-weight target in the new
enumeration. The accompanying surface appendix supplies a direct proof
and a separate dilation identity.

The continuous mean bound 2/3 for no-interior ideals does not extend to
arbitrary one-corner geometry. Exact counterexamples are preserved in the
earlier checkpoint. They are not counterexamples to Wilf: they need not
admit residue-bijective labels.

Ordinary bounds on the number of planar mixed corners, even including
bounds on those beyond the interior corner's projection, do not by
themselves restore that continuous inequality. The sixfold dilation of
the symmetric pair-cap example passes those counts and still fails the
2/3 bound. Congruence-capacity and erosion arguments exclude its arithmetic
realizability. The exact fixture and checks are preserved.

Equal weights are not a general justification for unequal weights.
The completed unit-weight theorem (cardinality>=65 implies D>=m) retains
its separate scope. At degree allowance 17, the stronger finite input
D_R>=4m fails: an exact (3,3,3)-corner example has m=858, D=3330 and
4m-D=102. The uniform-gap proof uses degrees 18 through 21 instead.

The new type-based reduction examined in this continuation is also
stronger than Wilf. Its established redundancy bound cannot simply be
substituted into its conjectured bound; a genuine four-generated example
with corner (1,7,4) disproves that shortcut. The source paper does not
claim to settle the missing inequality.
