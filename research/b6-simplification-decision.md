# D6: retain the strip and interval premises

## Decision and scope

Retain B6-strip-FV and B6-high-FV as specified in
[the high-height proof note](../paper/residual-high-height.md).
Their analytic implications are reconstructed; neither computed premise
is established by this decision. R6a and R6b must each supply two fresh
complete checking paths.

The retained route uses exact upper bounds from one structural theorem,
not sampled weights, numerical optimization, semigroup scans, or saved
witness lists. The strip transfers to a continuous \(5/42\) surplus.
That surplus and the phase inequality justify the high-height tree's
necessary-failure clipping; the tree supplies the final \(29/10\) allowance.

## Bounded simplification assessment

The no-full-corner continuous \(1/3\) bound cannot simply be applied after
deleting a positive excluded orthant: the completed ideal can have points
outside the original weighted-height simplex. Its height allowance changes,
so this would not prove the stated surplus for the clipped ideal.

The local and axis formulas remain valid lower bounds, but no analytic
inequality covering every residual high-height shape has been proved.
Likewise the planar short-corner partition is tied to a coordinate of
size two and does not supply a uniform replacement for arbitrary
positive corners. A more general argument would need new uniform
control of the clipped section moments, not a citation to a known
L-shape construction.

The finite strip gives that control on an explicitly bounded class
without residue labels. No upstream theorem presently audited supplies
its exact positive surplus. Chomicz remains relevant to the L-shape
interface, not a premise removing either finite contract.
Retaining the route is not a claim that analytic simplification is impossible.

## Artifact consequences

Keep one high-height subdivision tree and no checkpoint, leaf log,
parameter scan, producer executable, optimization cache, or old replay
comparison as required proof data. Strip bounds are freshly recomputed;
their saved values are diagnostics. The two mathematical evaluators must
remain independent even if administration and release packaging are shared.
For release, normalize the tree to topology/split data if that can be done
with a separately checked coverage-preserving conversion; cached scores
need not be carried into the standalone packet.
