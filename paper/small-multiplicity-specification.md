# The finite generator-box specification

## Status and purpose

This note completes roadmap task B1.3. It states the exact finite proposition
needed for multiplicities \(20\) through \(29\), proves that its domain contains
every possible counterexample, and gives code-independent acceptance
semantics for two independent checking paths.

This is a **specification and coverage proof**, not a replay result. It does
not promote the historical programs or their stored outputs into the proof.
The D1 simplification gate retained this computation. R1a has audited and
freshly run the residue-distance implementation; R1b must still audit and run
the independent ordinary-membership path.

## 1. The finite obligation

For each integer

\[
20\le m\le29,
\qquad
B_m=m(m-2),
\]

let \(\mathcal R_m\) be the raw set of strictly increasing triples

\[
\mathcal R_m
=
\{(a,b,c)\in\mathbb Z^3:m<a<b<c\le B_m\}.
\tag{1}
\]

For such a triple, write

\[
S(a,b,c)=\langle m,a,b,c\rangle.
\]

Call the triple *valid* when

\[
a\notin\langle m\rangle,
\qquad
b\notin\langle m,a\rangle,
\qquad
c\notin\langle m,a,b\rangle,
\tag{2}
\]

and

\[
\gcd(m,a,b,c)=1.
\tag{3}
\]

For a valid triple, let

\[
d_r=\min\{s\in S(a,b,c):s\equiv r\pmod m\},
\qquad 0\le r<m,
\tag{4}
\]

and put

\[
M=\max_r d_r.
\tag{5}
\]

The necessary subdomain is

\[
\mathcal Q_m
=
\{(a,b,c)\in\mathcal R_m:
   (2)\text{ and }(3)\text{ hold, and }M\le B_m\}.
\tag{6}
\]

The finite statement that a retained checker must establish is

> **Finite obligation B1.3-FV.** For every \(20\le m\le29\) and every
> \((a,b,c)\in\mathcal Q_m\),
> \[
> W_4(\langle m,a,b,c\rangle)\ge0.
> \tag{FV}
> \]

The weak inequality is essential: equality is an acceptable outcome.
Both endpoints \(m=20\), \(m=29\), and \(c=B_m\) are included. A checker may prove
the stronger statement for every valid triple in \(\mathcal R_m\), but only
(FV) is required by the global proof.

## 2. Canonical tuples and minimal generation

### Lemma 2.1 (numerical-semigroup criterion)

The positive monoid \(\langle m,a,b,c\rangle\) has finite complement in
\(\mathbb N\) if and only if \(\gcd(m,a,b,c)=1\).

#### Proof

If the gcd is greater than one, every element of the monoid is divisible by
that gcd, so its complement is infinite.

Conversely, suppose the gcd is one. The residues of \(a,b,c\) generate all of
the finite group \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). In a finite group, the submonoid
generated by a set is the subgroup it generates, because the inverse of an
element of order \(h\) is its \((h-1)\)-fold sum. Thus every residue \(r\) modulo
\(m\) has a representative \(d_r\) which is a nonnegative combination of
\(a,b,c\). If \(D=\max_r d_r\), then every integer \(n\ge D\) is
\(d_r+qm\) for its residue \(r\) and some \(q\ge0\). Hence every such \(n\) belongs
to the monoid. \(\square\)

### Lemma 2.2 (prefix minimality)

For a tuple in \(\mathcal R_m\), conditions (2) are equivalent to
\(\{m,a,b,c\}\) being the minimal generating set of
\(\langle m,a,b,c\rangle\).

#### Proof

The generator \(m\) cannot be generated by the other three, since each is
larger than \(m\). If \(a\) is generated by \(m,b,c\), the coefficients of \(b\) and
\(c\) must be zero, so \(a\in\langle m\rangle\). Similarly, a representation
of \(b\) by the other generators cannot use \(c\), and hence is exactly a
representation in \(\langle m,a\rangle\). Redundancy of \(c\) is exactly
membership in \(\langle m,a,b\rangle\). This proves both directions.
\(\square\)

### Proposition 2.3 (canonical parametrization)

Valid triples in \(\mathcal R_m\) are in bijection with minimally
four-generated numerical semigroups of multiplicity \(m\) whose largest
minimal generator is at most \(B_m\).

#### Proof

Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2 show that a valid tuple generates a numerical semigroup
with embedding dimension four. Its multiplicity is \(m\), since its other
generators are larger than \(m\).

Conversely, a numerical semigroup has a unique minimal generating set: its
positive elements which cannot be expressed as sums of two positive
semigroup elements. For embedding dimension four and multiplicity \(m\), sort
the other three elements to obtain a unique tuple \(m<a<b<c\). If \(c\le B_m\),
that tuple lies in \(\mathcal R_m\) and satisfies (2)--(3). \(\square\)

There are exactly \(B_m-m\) allowable integers in
\(\{m+1,\ldots,B_m\}\), so

\[
|\mathcal R_m|=\binom{B_m-m}{3}.
\tag{7}
\]

Equivalently, a canonical nested traversal has the inclusive bounds

\[
m+1\le a\le B_m-2,\qquad
a+1\le b\le B_m-1,\qquad
b+1\le c\le B_m.
\]

Summing (7) for \(m=20,\ldots,29\) gives \(301{,}098{,}092\). This number is a
useful traversal diagnostic, not a proof that an implementation visited the
right tuples.

## 3. Coverage of every possible counterexample

We use two previously reconstructed facts. First, as also recorded in the
[foundational note](foundations.md#3-preferred-exponent-representatives-f1),
a nonmultiplicity minimal generator \(u\) belongs to
\(\operatorname{Ap}(S,m)\): otherwise
\(u-m\in S\), and

\[
u=m+(u-m)
\]

would express \(u\) as a sum of two positive semigroup elements. Consequently
every such generator is at most
\(M=\max\operatorname{Ap}(S,m)\).

Second, the
[negative-case conductor reduction](conductor-reduction.md) proves

\[
W_4(S)<0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M\le m(m-2)=B_m.
\tag{8}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (exhaustive counterexample coverage)

If \(S\) is a numerical semigroup of embedding dimension four,
\(20\le m(S)\le29\), and \(W_4(S)<0\), then its sorted nonmultiplicity
generators form an element of \(\mathcal Q_{m(S)}\).

#### Proof

Write the unique increasing minimal generator list as

\[
S=\langle m,a,b,c\rangle.
\]

By (8), \(M\le B_m\). Each of \(a,b,c\) is an Apéry element, so

\[
m<a<b<c\le M\le B_m.
\]

Thus \((a,b,c)\in\mathcal R_m\). Because this is the minimal generating set,
(2) holds; because \(S\) is numerical, (3) holds. The same bound \(M\le B_m\)
then places the tuple in \(\mathcal Q_m\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (sufficiency of the finite obligation)

If (FV) is verified, every numerical semigroup of embedding dimension four
and multiplicity between \(20\) and \(29\) satisfies Wilf's inequality.

#### Proof

A counterexample would belong to some \(\mathcal Q_m\) by Theorem 3.1 and
would contradict (FV). \(\square\)

This is a one-way negative-case reduction. It does not imply that semigroups
outside the box, or valid boxed tuples with \(M>B_m\), have positive Wilf
number. In particular, a positive minimum found inside the checked domain
cannot be advertised as a global strict-positivity result.

## 4. Exact acceptance predicates

Every raw tuple must be accounted for once. A convenient disjoint
classification applies the following tests in order:

1. \(a\in\langle m\rangle\);
2. after the first test fails, \(b\in\langle m,a\rangle\);
3. after the first two fail, \(c\in\langle m,a,b\rangle\);
4. after all redundancy tests fail, \(\gcd(m,a,b,c)>1\);
5. otherwise, the tuple is valid.

The first test is equivalently \(a\equiv0\pmod m\). Later, larger generators
cannot make an earlier generator redundant, so rejecting every completion of
a failed prefix is exact. The final gcd test cannot in general be moved to an
earlier prefix: adjoining a later generator can reduce the gcd to one.

For a valid tuple, define

\[
\Sigma=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}d_r.
\]

The standard Apéry identities, reconstructed in the
[foundational note](foundations.md#4-the-moment-identity-and-baseline-f2),
give the conductor \(C\), genus \(g\), and Wilf number as

\[
C=M-m+1,
\qquad
g=\frac{\Sigma}{m}-\frac{m-1}{2},
\tag{9}
\]

\[
W_4=3C-4g
=
\frac{3mM-4\Sigma-m(m-1)}{m}.
\tag{10}
\]

Thus one exact acceptance predicate for a valid tuple is

\[
M>B_m
\quad\text{or}\quad
3mM-4\Sigma-m(m-1)\ge0.
\tag{11}
\]

The numerator in (10) must be divisible by \(m\). A retained checker must test
this divisibility rather than rely on truncating division.

An equivalent direct-membership predicate avoids Apéry shortest paths. Let
\(I(n)\) denote membership in \(S(a,b,c)\). Then

\[
M\le B_m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[B_m-m+1,B_m]\subseteq S(a,b,c).
\tag{12}
\]

Indeed, the interval in (12) contains the largest integer at most \(B_m\) in
each residue class. If \(M\le B_m\), the minimum representative \(d_r\) in each
class lies no later than its interval representative, which is therefore
\(d_r+qm\in S\). The converse is immediate because the interval supplies a
representative at most \(B_m\) for every residue.

When the window in (12) is full, every integer above \(B_m\) is also in \(S\):
subtract a suitable positive multiple of \(m\) to reach the representative in
that window. Hence membership on \([0,B_m]\) determines the full genus and
conductor. The equivalent exact test is then

\[
W_4=3C-4g\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]

Failure to fill the window proves \(M>B_m\) and places the valid tuple outside
the necessary domain. It is not a failure of (FV).

## 5. Required generation and checking behavior

A conforming verification must:

1. generate every integer \(m=20,\ldots,29\);
2. generate every triple in \(\mathcal R_m\) once, with the inclusive upper
   endpoint \(B_m\);
3. classify it by the exact prefix-membership and gcd predicates above;
4. for every valid tuple, establish exactly whether \(M\le B_m\);
5. for every tuple in \(\mathcal Q_m\), evaluate (10) or (13) exactly and
   reject the run if \(W_4<0\);
6. reject malformed input, unsupported ranges, arithmetic failure,
   unclassified tuples, or an unresolved membership or residue computation.

There are no symmetry quotients beyond the unique increasing order, no
probabilistic filters, and no floating-point predicates. Counts, checksums,
minimizers, and elapsed time may be recorded, but none is an acceptance
criterion.

All relevant bounds are small, though they must still be documented by an
implementation. Here \(B_m\le783\). In the residue graph on \(m\) vertices, a
least-weight path can be chosen without a repeated vertex because all edge
weights are positive. Therefore

\[
0\le d_r\le(m-1)B_m,
\qquad
0\le\Sigma\le m(m-1)B_m.
\tag{14}
\]

The right side of the second bound is at most \(635{,}796\). These estimates
also give

\[
\left|3mM-4\Sigma-m(m-1)\right|
\le 7m(m-1)B_m+m(m-1)
\le4{,}451{,}384.
\tag{15}
\]

They make signed 64-bit arithmetic more than sufficient for the displayed
quantities; an implementation must separately bound any sentinel or packed
representation it introduces. Alternatively, the final checker may use
arbitrary-precision integers.

## 6. Independent checking paths

D1 retains (FV). The two replay paths have distinct mathematical interfaces.

### R1a: residue distances

Use the directed residue graph with vertices \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) and,
for each generator \(w\in\{a,b,c\}\), an edge

\[
r\longrightarrow r+w\pmod m
\]

of integer weight \(w\). Exact shortest distances from residue zero are the
values \(d_r\) in (4). Prefix versions of the same calculation decide (2).
Explicitly, a proposed generator \(w\) belongs to its prefix semigroup exactly
when the prefix distance to \(w\bmod m\) is at most \(w\). The checker then
applies (5), (10), and (11).

R1a must prove that its concrete shortest-path update, infinity convention,
loop bounds, and integer types implement this abstract graph calculation.
The completed
[R1a audit and replay](../verification/b1/r1a-residue-distance-audit.md)
discharges this path without treating stored historical output as proof.

### R1b: ordinary membership

Compute membership on \([0,B_m]\) in increasing integer order. When adjoining
a generator \(w\), use

\[
I_j(n)=I_{j-1}(n)
\vee
\bigl(n\ge w\text{ and }I_j(n-w)\bigr),
\tag{16}
\]

where \(I_j\) permits the first \(j\) generators. This recurrence decides the
prefix tests and full membership without residue distances. Apply the window
equivalence (12), then count the gaps and locate the last gap to evaluate
(13).

R1b must independently generate the raw tuples. It must not trust R1a's
accepted-tuple list, Apéry data, classifications, or cached successes.
Agreement statistics between the two paths are useful diagnostics, but each
path must establish its own coverage and predicate.

The two paths need to agree only on the required domain \(\mathcal Q_m\). A
residue implementation may additionally evaluate every valid boxed tuple
with \(M>B_m\); that stronger historical check is not part of (FV).

## 7. What this task does and does not establish

B1.3 discharges the mathematical specification and exhaustive-coverage
obligation for the small-multiplicity branch. Together with B1.2 it proves
that (FV) is sufficient.

It does **not** establish (FV), validate stored counts, audit source code, or
record a fresh replay. The
[D1 gate](../research/b1-simplification-decision.md) retained (FV), so the two
checking paths must be implemented and replayed under the
[verification trust policy](../verification/trust-policy.md). R1a is complete;
R1b and the final agreement audit remain.
